# UK Energy Price Cap: Seasonal Increases by Year (2020–2026)

## Overview

The Ofgem energy price cap limits the rates for customers on standard variable energy tariffs.
Originally changed twice a year (summer/winter), from October 2022 it changes quarterly (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct).

> **Note:** Ofgem changed its Typical Domestic Consumption Values (TDCVs) in October 2023,
> reducing assumed typical gas consumption from 12,000 kWh to 11,500 kWh and electricity from
> 2,900 kWh to 2,700 kWh. This makes direct comparisons of headline figures complex.

## 2020

| Period         | Cap Level (approx.) | Change     |
|----------------|---------------------|------------|
| Apr–Sep 2020   | ~£1,126/yr          | Decrease   |
| Oct 2020–Mar 2021 | ~£1,042/yr       | Decrease   |

## 2021

| Period         | Cap Level (approx.) | Change         |
|----------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Apr–Sep 2021   | ~£1,138/yr          | Small increase |
| Oct 2021–Mar 2022 | ~£1,277/yr       | +12% increase  |

Global gas prices started rising from summer 2021 as economies reopened post-pandemic.

## 2022

| Period         | Cap Level (approx.)                          | Change     |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------|------------|
| Apr–Sep 2022   | ~£1,971/yr                                    | **+54%**   |
| Oct–Dec 2022   | Cap: ~£3,549/yr (EPG limited bills to ~£2,500) | **+80%** (capped at +27% by govt) |

The Energy Price Guarantee (EPG) was introduced Oct 2022 to cap bills at £2,500/yr.

---

## Summary of Year-on-Year Seasonal Increases

| Year | Biggest Seasonal Increase | Notes |
|------|---------------------------|-------|
| 2020 | None (prices fell)        | Pandemic reduced demand |
| 2021 | Oct cap: +12% (~£139)     | Post-pandemic recovery, gas prices rising |
| 2022 | Apr cap: **+54%** (~£693) | Energy crisis — Russia/Ukraine conflict |
| 2023 | Jan cap: +14% (but EPG protected consumers) | Peak crisis, govt intervention |
| 2024 | Oct cap: +10% (~£149)     | Modest winter increase |
| 2025 | Apr cap: +6% (~£111)      | Moderate spring increase |
| 2026 | Jan cap: +0.2% (~£3)      | Near-stable, then Apr decrease |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Even with the April 2026 decrease, prices remain **~35% above pre-crisis levels**
- The biggest seasonal increase was **+54% in April 2022** during the energy crisis
- Government intervention (EPG) shielded consumers from the full Oct 2022 cap of £3,549
- Since Q3 2023, the cap has broadly stabilised in the £1,600–£1,900 range
- Next announcements: 27 May 2026 (Q3 2026) and 26 August 2026 (Q4 2026)
